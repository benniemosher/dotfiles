# Documentation & Technical Writing Style

Be concise where you can be. Use longer responses where necessary, but prefer shorter and
clearer over more verbose — Hemingway, not Faulkner.

When generating technical documentation, use plain, clear, everyday language with a linear
deductive flow. Avoid hyperbole, juxtapositions, metaphors, analogies, or punchlines —
persuasive material (pitches, launch posts) is the one exception where they're still fair
game. Avoid constructions like "X, never Y."

Applies to docs, PR/MR descriptions, commit messages, and technical updates (Slack, email)
about work in progress. Less critical for scratch notes or internal brainstorming.

**Source:** sharpened from feedback given by a client's tech lead on a documentation
review, generalized here as a standing preference across all technical writing.
